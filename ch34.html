Part 6: The lab, their papers, the committee

Chapter 34 of 45

How to behave in front of the panel (online)

When and how long: Sat 21 Nov, 10 minutes.

One page for the exam itself. For names and general etiquette, see 12_Day_of/05_how_to_address_and_etiquette.md. Sources are numbered [1] to [7] at the bottom.

1. Before they speak

  • Have your Examination Card ready (on UCARO from 12:00 JST on 19 Nov). Your UCARO photo is used to identify you. Details of the exam are sent on the day [1].
  • Join early. Camera on, quiet room, phone silent, your name shown correctly on Teams or Zoom.
  • Expect a few faculty who have already read your documents [2]. One person will usually lead. Let that person run the session.

2. Greeting

  • Wait until someone speaks to you. Then: "Good afternoon. My name is Sudhanshu Pandit, examinee number [M____]. Thank you very much for this opportunity." A small nod is enough.
  • Optional, only if it comes out smoothly: "Yoroshiku onegai itashimasu." (よろしくお願いいたします). In Japanese interviews you give your name and then say this line [3].
  • If they ask you to introduce yourself, keep it to about one minute [2].

3. Addressing each person

  • In English, "Professor" + family name is safe for everyone on the panel, associate and assistant professors included: "Professor Zheng", "Professor Okamura".
  • On official lists the family name comes first in capitals (ZHENG Junjun), so the family name is Zheng.
  • Not sure of a name? Don't guess. "Thank you for the question" works.
  • "-sensei" (Zheng-sensei) only if they use it themselves on the call. Never "-san" for a professor [4].

4. If you didn't hear, or don't understand

  • Didn't hear: don't guess an answer [5]. "Sorry, the audio broke up. Could you repeat the last part, please?" In Japanese: "Osoreirimasu ga, saigo no bubun o mō ichido onegai dekimasu deshō ka." (恐れ入りますが、最後の部分をもう一度お願いできますでしょうか。)
  • Heard it but not sure what they mean: check first. "Just to check, are you asking whether ...?" Then answer.
  • You both start talking: stop. "Sorry, please go ahead." (失礼いたしました。先にお話しいただけますか。) [5]
  • You don't know the answer: "I don't know yet. I'd start by ..." Plain and short.
  • Connection drops: rejoin the same link at once. If the chat works, type one short line ("Audio dropped, rejoining"). If only the video freezes, ask whether they can still hear you and wait for their instructions [5]. Keep the Support Office contact (guideline p. 8) open in another tab.

5. If a question comes in Japanese

The program teaches 100% in English [6], so asking to answer in English is fine. Say it politely:

  • You understood it: "Mōshiwake gozaimasen. Nihongo wa mada benkyō-chū desu node, eigo de o-kotae shite mo yoroshii deshō ka." (申し訳ございません。日本語はまだ勉強中ですので、英語でお答えしてもよろしいでしょうか。) = "I'm sorry, I'm still learning Japanese. May I answer in English?"
  • You didn't understand it: "Mōshiwake gozaimasen. Mō ichido eigo de onegai dekimasu deshō ka." (申し訳ございません。もう一度英語でお願いできますでしょうか。) = "I'm sorry. Could you say it once more in English, please?"
  • "Do you speak Japanese?": answer honestly, as in 04_My_Proposal/03_weak_points_and_honest_answers.md (beginner, below N5, studying).

6. Ending

  • When the person leading says it is over: "Thank you very much for your time today." Optional: "Honjitsu wa arigatō gozaimashita." (本日はありがとうございました。)
  • Follow their instructions for leaving. If there are none, say "Shitsurei itashimasu" (失礼いたします) or "Thank you, goodbye", leave, and check that the call has really ended [5].
  • Don't ask how you did. Results come by email, planned for 14 Dec 2026 [7].
