Part 1: The meeting and previous years' questions

Chapter 5 of 62

Topics that repeat across the previous years' questions

When and how long: 15 minutes, then use it to pick questions alongside Part 5.

This page counts the topics in the seven question sets in this folder: the six sets of the Informatics and Data Science Program from August 2023 to January 2026, and the older Information Engineering sample set (about 2014 to 2016). Most questions in your meeting will be on your plan (Prof. Zheng, 2 Nov). A professor may still ask a basic maths or informatics question out loud, and these sets show what this faculty treats as basic knowledge.

How to read the table. I-Q3(2) means Part I, Question 3, part (2). A topic counts once per set, however many parts it has. "Sets" is out of 7. The older set comes from a different program and is about ten years older, so a topic that only shows up there matters less.

The layout is the same in every program set

All six program sets (Aug 2023 to Jan 2026) use the same slots:

  • Part I: Q1 linear algebra, Q2 calculus in two variables, Q3 probability, Q4 discrete maths (graphs, sets and relations, predicate logic).
  • Part II: Q1 information or coding theory, Q2 algorithms and data structures (graph colouring in Jan 2024), Q3 C programming, Q4 digital circuits (an 8-bit floating-point format in Jan 2025), Q5 statistics, Q6 machine learning, Q7 a short summary of your research.

So every slot repeats in 6 of 6 sets. The table below goes one level down, to the topics inside the slots. The older set has six questions in each part, in a different order, so its column uses its own numbers.

What to study first (for a software reliability lab)

The order mixes two things: how often a topic appears above, and how close it is to the lab's work (failure times, failure counts, reliability growth models, Markov chains). Paths are relative to this file.

  1. Probability distributions: mean, variance and other moments. In all 7 sets (Part I Q3 every time). The ones that keep coming back are the exponential, Poisson, gamma and normal, which are also the lab's models for failure times and failure counts. Questions: I-Q3 in every set, plus Aug 2024 II-Q5(2)-(3) and Jan 2026 I-Q3(4)-(5) for moments and a mean that does not exist. Notes: 09_Math/02_probability.md sections 1.2 and 1.3, P6 to P11, P13, P16 to P18 and P25; 09_Math/01_reliability_and_probability_primer.md section 1; 09_Math/01_linear_algebra_and_calculus.md L8, L9 and L14 (the integrals behind E[X], E[X²] and the Gamma function); 09_Math/04_stochastic_processes_and_reliability.md R18 (MTTF is the integral of R).

  2. The distribution of a maximum, minimum, sum, or Y = g(X). In 6 of 7 sets. In reliability terms, the minimum of lifetimes is a series system, the maximum is a parallel system, and a sum is the time to the n-th failure. Questions: Jan 2024 I-Q3(3)-(5), Aug 2024 I-Q3(3), Jan 2025 I-Q3(5), Aug 2025 I-Q3(4), Jan 2026 II-Q5(3), older set I-Q3(3)-(4). Notes: 09_Math/02_probability.md section 1.5, P11 and P14 to P17 (P17(c) gives the density n x^(n−1) of a maximum, as in Jan 2026 II-Q5(3)); 09_Math/04_stochastic_processes_and_reliability.md R20 and R21. 09_Math has no worked change-of-variable problem (the density of Y = X²), so use the answers to Jan 2025 I-Q3(5), Aug 2025 I-Q3(4) and older set I-Q3(3)-(4).

  3. Maximum likelihood. In every set since Aug 2024 (normal, exponential, uniform). The lab fits its software reliability growth models (NHPP, Goel-Okumoto) this way. Questions: Aug 2024 II-Q5(1), Aug 2025 II-Q5, Jan 2026 II-Q5. Notes: 09_Math/03_statistics.md section 1.1 and S4 to S7; 09_Math/04_stochastic_processes_and_reliability.md R7 and R8; 09_Math/05_math_inside_my_research_plan.md D10. Remember the uniform case in Jan 2026: the maximum is at the edge of the allowed range, so setting the derivative to zero does not find it.

  4. Eigenvalues first, then rank, null space and determinant. Eigenvalues in 6 of 7 sets, rank or null space in 5, a determinant, inverse or matrix power in 3. Markov chain and availability models are read from eigenvalues and matrix powers. Questions: I-Q1 in every set. Notes: 09_Math/01_linear_algebra_and_calculus.md Part 1 (the linear algebra table), L1, L4 and L5; 09_Math/from_NAIST/02_practice_problems_with_solutions.md LA7, LA8 (rank with a parameter, like Jan 2025 I-Q1), LA10, LA16, LA19, LA20, LA22 and LA23.

  5. Calculus in two variables. In all 7 sets (Part I Q2 every time): extrema with the Hessian or Lagrange multipliers (4 sets), limits (4), the Maclaurin expansion (4), double integrals (3). Fitting a two-parameter model means finding where the gradient of the log-likelihood is zero and checking the Hessian. Questions: I-Q2 in every set. Notes: 09_Math/01_linear_algebra_and_calculus.md Part 1 (the calculus table), L6, L7 and L12 to L15; 09_Math/from_NAIST/02_practice_problems_with_solutions.md C2, C20 and C21. 09_Math has no worked two-variable Maclaurin or two-variable limit problem; the answers to Aug 2023 I-Q2, Aug 2025 I-Q2 and older set I-Q2 fill that gap.

  6. Statistics: tests, intervals and least squares. Part II Q5 is statistics in all 6 program sets; tests or intervals appear in 3 sets and regression in 2. Comparing the reliability of two versions needs a test and an interval. Questions: Aug 2023 II-Q5, Jan 2024 II-Q5, Jan 2025 II-Q5, older set I-Q4. Notes: 09_Math/03_statistics.md sections 1.2 to 1.4, S9, S10, S14, S16 and S20.

  7. Machine learning in plain words. Part II Q6 in all 6 program sets: explain a method in about 100 words (4 sets), logistic regression (3), the derivative of the sigmoid or softmax (2), a confusion matrix or a Bayes decision with a loss (2). Questions: II-Q6 in the six program sets. Notes: 09_Math/03_statistics.md section 1.4 and S21 (logistic regression); 09_Math/02_probability.md section 1.1 and P3 (Bayes). 09_Math has nothing on softmax, cross-validation, overfitting, or precision and recall, so use the worked answers in the question files.

  8. Discrete maths. Part I Q4 in all 6 program sets: predicate logic (3 sets), graphs (3), relations (2), injective and surjective functions (2), counting (2). Questions: I-Q4 in the six program sets, older set I-Q5. Notes: 09_Math/06_cs_fundamentals_quick_review.md sections 6 and 9; 09_Math/02_probability.md P1 (inclusion-exclusion).

  9. Algorithms, data structures and C. A C program in all 7 sets, running time in 5, stacks, queues and lists in 5, sorting in 4, trees and heaps in 3. Out loud this comes as a short question; others were asked "What is a struct in C, and why use it?" (see 02_questions_others_were_asked.md). Questions: II-Q2 and II-Q3 in the six program sets, older set I-Q6 and II-Q3. Notes: 09_Math/06_cs_fundamentals_quick_review.md sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 and its 20 short Q&As. 09_Math has no note on C.

  10. Information theory and digital circuits. Part II Q1 and Q4 in all 6 program sets: entropy (4 sets), source extensions (3), prefix codes and Huffman codes (3), two's complement and adders (3). These are furthest from the lab's work, so they come last. Questions: II-Q1 and II-Q4 in the six program sets, older set II-Q1 and II-Q4. Notes: only 09_Math/06_cs_fundamentals_quick_review.md section 10 (floating point) touches this, so the worked answers in the question files are the material.
