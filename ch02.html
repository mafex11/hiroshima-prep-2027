Chapter 2 of 45

The oral exam: what we know about the format

When and how long: Fri 13 Nov, 15 minutes. Send the three questions when Prof. Zheng's email arrives.

Everything here comes from three places. Each fact is tagged with its source.

  • [Guideline p.N] The official guideline, April 2027 Admission, Special Selection for International Students [For Students Living Outside Japan], Application Guidebook (September 2026). The PDF is in this folder: Hiroshima_Application_Guideline_April_2027.pdf. Page numbers are the printed ones, which match the PDF pages.
  • [Zheng, 2 Nov] Your Teams call with Assoc. Prof. Junjun Zheng on 2 Nov 2026, 13:52 to 13:54 IST. See zheng_meeting_2_nov_transcript.md. The speech-to-text is rough. "50 minutes" in it was really "15". You checked "so, like half an hour?" and he said "Yes".
  • [Office, 12 Nov] The Support Office email of 12 Nov 2026.

Things I worked out myself, rather than read somewhere, are marked [my inference].

The short version

  • One online oral exam, on one day between Tue 24 Nov and Wed 2 Dec 2026 (Japan time). There is no written exam in your selection.
  • About 30 minutes: about 10 minutes presenting your past work, about 5 minutes on your research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions.
  • The committee grades the oral exam A, B or C. Your documents are also graded A, B or C. The two grades are judged together.
  • The exact details are only given on the day. Prof. Zheng will send the schedule.

What we know, fact by fact

Date and time

  • The exam is on "a day specified by the head of the Program between Tuesday, November 24, 2026 and Wednesday, December 2, 2026". All times in the guideline are Japan Standard Time. [Guideline p.19]
  • "The date of the entrance examination will be notified by the respective Programs." [Guideline p.19]
  • "Further details regarding the examination schedule and other arrangements will be provided by your prospective supervisor." So the date will come from Prof. Zheng, not from the Support Office. [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Japan is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India. A 10:00 JST slot is 06:30 in Bangalore. [my inference]

How it is run

  • "Your academic abilities regarding knowledge in the specialized field, application capability, and problem-solving abilities will be evaluated by the oral examination via internet using remote conferencing systems such as Teams or Zoom." [Guideline p.19]
  • The Japanese edition says the same: 「Teams，Zoom 等の遠隔会議システムを利用した口述試験を行い，専門分野の知識や応用力及び課題解決能力等に関する学力を検査します。」 ("We hold an oral exam on a remote system such as Teams or Zoom and test academic ability in specialised knowledge, application and problem solving.") Source: the April 2026 Japanese guideline for the same selection, section VI.2, https://www.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/system/files/270784/M%E5%A4%96%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%EF%BC%88%E5%9B%BD%E5%A4%96%EF%BC%89%E5%8B%9F%E9%9B%86%E8%A6%81%E9%A0%85%EF%BC%88%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E7%89%88%EF%BC%89202604.pdf
  • "Details of the oral examination will be notified on the day of the entrance examination." [Guideline p.19]
  • The photo you uploaded to UCARO "will be used for identification at the examination". Expect them to check your face against it. [Guideline p.12]

Structure of the 30 minutes

  • About 10 minutes presenting "your research", then about 5 minutes on the research proposal. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:52:50] His words: "you have to present your research about 10 minutes, ... and 5 minutes for a research proposal."
  • Then about 15 minutes of questions, so about 30 minutes in total. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:05 to 13:53:17]
  • On what the questions cover: "Maybe not limited to research proposal ... it depends on the committee members." [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:35 to 13:53:42]
  • He expects "Why did you choose Japan?" and "Why did you choose Hiroshima University?" [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:48 to 13:53:52]
  • "I think maybe most of QA will focus on the research proposal. ... Don't worry, it's okay." [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:54:05]
  • You asked whether maths, statistics or algebra would come up. He did not say no. He said it depends on the committee. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:21 to 13:53:57]

What the committee will have in front of them

  • Your application documents: transcript, degree certificate, research plan, two recommendation letters, English score (IELTS), Personal History, the List for the Oral Examination (口述試験調書) and the Consent Form. [Guideline p.16 to 17]
  • The List for the Oral Examination is the form they read just before talking to you. Yours covers your reason for choosing Hiroshima, hackathons and side projects, the 2026 studies that "impressed you recently", hobbies and skills, your jobs, and the 2023 paper and award. Your copy is 04_My_Proposal/List_for_Oral_Examination_SUBMITTED.pdf. In one Hiroshima transfer-exam report (B8 in file 02), the interviewers asked about what the applicant wrote on this kind of form. So expect a question on anything you wrote there. [my inference, from file 02]

How you are graded

  • "The results of the oral examination & the screening of the application documents" are judged together. [Guideline p.19]
  • Oral examination: a scale of 1 to 3 (A, B, C). Application documents: a scale of 1 to 3 (A, B, C). [Guideline p.19]
  • Results are planned for Monday 14 December 2026, by email to accepted applicants only. They do not answer phone inquiries. [Guideline p.19]
  • After the result you can ask to see your own grades (oral exam and documents) using a disclosure form. [Guideline p.21]
  • Your earlier talks with Prof. Zheng "do not affect the result of the selection in any way". [Guideline p.20]

Before the day

  • Your application is complete. [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Your examinee number and Examination Card appear on UCARO after 12:00 JST on 19 Nov 2026 (08:30 IST). "Please be sure to download and prepare your Examination Card from UCARO so that you can recognize it on the day of the examination." [Guideline p.13] and [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Learn your examinee number by heart. In four separate reports from Hiroshima (one on this program, three on the informatics transfer exam), the first question was "What is your examinee number?" See file 02.

Misconduct

  • "If you engage in misconduct, you will be instructed to discontinue the examination and will not be permitted to take any subsequent examinations. Furthermore, if misconduct is discovered after admission or enrollment, admission and/or enrollment will be revoked." Fees already paid are not refunded. [Guideline p.20]

Warning: no AI help of any kind during the exam

On 2 Nov you ran Natively during the call with Prof. Zheng. It listened to the call and showed you suggested answers, and you read some of them out (see zheng_meeting_2_nov_review.md). Doing that in the exam would be misconduct. The guideline does not list every banned act, but an app that hears the questions and writes your answers is help from outside, which is what cheating means. NAIST's 2027 guide names "receiving assistance from others" as improper conduct for its online interviews. Hiroshima would see it the same way. The cost is not only this exam: under the guideline, admission can be taken away even after you enrol, if it comes out later.

On the day:

  • Quit Natively fully (not just hide it). Check it is not set to start on its own or to join Teams or Zoom calls.
  • Close every AI assistant and meeting note-taker: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, any transcription or "meeting notes" app, and browser extensions that do the same.
  • Do not record the exam unless they say you may. [my advice; the guideline does not mention recording]
  • Keep only what they allow on screen. Ask first if you want your research plan PDF open (see the questions below).
  • Practise without Natively from now on, so your answers are your own habit, not something you read.

What we don't know yet

  • The exact date and time.
  • Teams or Zoom. (The guideline says "such as Teams or Zoom". Prof. Zheng used Teams on 2 Nov.)
  • Who is on the committee and how many people. Prof. Zheng will probably be there, but that is not confirmed. Prof. Dohi and Prof. Okamura are possible, but nothing says so.
  • Whether you may share slides on screen, and whether they want the slides sent beforehand.
  • What "your research" means for the first 10 minutes: your graduation project, your 2023 review paper, or your work with coding agents at Layerpath.
  • Whether there will be maths or basic informatics questions, and in what form (asked aloud, or a problem shown on screen).
  • Whether you may keep your research plan or notes open.
  • Whether there is a connection test before the day, and what happens if your connection drops.
  • The language. Everything so far has been in English, and the guideline is in English, but nothing says the exam must be.

Questions to ask Prof. Zheng when his scheduling email arrives

Keep it to these three. They are short and he can answer each in a line.

  1. The split. "Is it about 10 minutes on my past work and 5 minutes on my research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions? For the past work, should I talk about my graduation project, or my work with AI coding agents at Layerpath?"
  2. Platform and slides. "Will it be on Teams or Zoom, and may I share slides on screen? Should I send them before the exam?"
  3. Materials. "May I keep a printed copy of my research plan with me during the questions? Is there anything else I should prepare?"

(Don't ask who is on the committee. Professors usually can't say, and it can sound like you want an advantage.)

If he seems happy to say more, one optional fourth: "Should I expect questions on basic mathematics, such as probability or linear algebra?"

A draft you can adapt (check 12_Day_of/04_email_templates.md for the tone the kit uses):

Dear Prof. Zheng,

Thank you for the schedule. I have three short questions so I can prepare well.

  1. Is the format about 10 minutes on my past work and 5 minutes on my research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions? For the past work, should I focus on my graduation project or on my work with AI coding agents?
  2. Will the exam be on Teams or Zoom, and may I share slides on screen?
  3. May I keep a printed copy of my research plan with me during the questions? Is there anything else I should prepare?

Best regards, Sudhanshu Pandit

How this compares with other exams (context only)

  • In the general selection for the same program, the oral exam comes the day after a written exam worth 600 points. It assesses "Motivation, aptitude for the desired subject of study and basic academic abilities" on a scale of 1 to 3. Source: 2025 General Selection A, English guideline, pp. 20 to 24, https://www.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/system/files/261531/M%E4%B8%80%E8%88%ACA%E6%97%A5%E7%A8%8B%E5%8B%9F%E9%9B%86%E8%A6%81%E9%A0%85%EF%BC%88%E8%8B%B1%E8%AA%9E%E7%89%88%EF%BC%892025.pdf
  • In your selection there is no written exam, so the oral exam is the only place they can check "knowledge in the specialized field". [my inference] That is a reason to be ready for a few basic maths or informatics questions, even though Prof. Zheng expects most questions to be on your plan. File 03 shows what this faculty treats as basic knowledge.
  • Similar online exams elsewhere: the University of Tokyo's international selection (Earth and Planetary Science) uses a 10-minute presentation of research background and plan plus 10 minutes of questions on Zoom. NAIST Information Science uses a 20-minute online interview with oral maths questions plus questions on the research proposal. Details and links are in file 02.
