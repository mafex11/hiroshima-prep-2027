Part 1: The meeting and previous years' questions

Chapter 2 of 59

Your meeting with the professors: what we know about the format

When and how long: Fri 13 Nov, 15 minutes. Send the three questions when Prof. Zheng's email arrives.

Most facts here come from three places: Hiroshima University itself, your call with Prof. Zheng, and the Support Office. Each fact is tagged with its source. The few from elsewhere, such as your list of enclosed documents, name their source in the line.

  • [Hiroshima] What Hiroshima University tells applicants on your route.
  • [Zheng, 2 Nov] Your Teams call with Assoc. Prof. Junjun Zheng on 2 Nov 2026 (13:30 to 13:56 IST). The part about the format is at 13:52 to 13:54. See zheng_meeting_2_nov_review.md. The speech-to-text of the call was rough. "50 minutes" in it was really "15". It also has a line "So, I have a one hour." But you then checked "so, like half an hour?" and he said "Yes".
  • [Office, 12 Nov] The Support Office email of 12 Nov 2026.

Things I worked out myself, rather than read somewhere, are marked [my inference].

The short version

  • One online meeting with the professors, on one day between Tue 24 Nov and Wed 2 Dec 2026 (Japan time). There is no written part in your route.
  • About 30 minutes: about 10 minutes presenting your past work, about 5 minutes on your research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions.
  • Prof. Zheng will send the details and the schedule.

What we know, fact by fact

Date and time

  • The meeting is on a day the head of the Program sets, between Tuesday 24 November and Wednesday 2 December 2026. All times are Japan Standard Time. [Hiroshima]
  • Each Program tells its own applicants the date. [Hiroshima]
  • Your prospective supervisor will send the schedule and the other details. So the date will come from Prof. Zheng, not from the Support Office. [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Japan is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India. A 10:00 JST slot is 06:30 in Bangalore. [my inference]

How it is run

  • It is held online, on a remote meeting system such as Teams or Zoom. [Hiroshima]
  • The details of how the meeting runs will also come from Prof. Zheng. [Office, 12 Nov]

Structure of the 30 minutes

  • About 10 minutes presenting "your research", then about 5 minutes on the research proposal. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:52:50] His words: "you have to present your research about 10 minutes, ... and 5 minutes for a research proposal."
  • Then about 15 minutes of questions, so about 30 minutes in total. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:05 to 13:53:17]
  • On what the questions cover: "Maybe not limited to research proposal ..." He added that it depends on which professors join. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:35 to 13:53:42]
  • He expects "Why did you choose Japan?" and "Why did you choose Hiroshima University?" [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:48 to 13:53:52]
  • "I think maybe most of QA will focus on the research proposal. ... Don't worry, it's okay." [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:54:05]
  • You asked whether maths, statistics or algebra would come up. He did not say no. He said it depends on which professors join. [Zheng, 2 Nov, 13:53:21 to 13:53:57]

What the professors will have in front of them

  • The documents your program asks for are the transcript, a certificate of graduation, the research plan, two recommendation letters, an English certificate such as IELTS or a certificate of English-medium study, Personal History, the form about yourself, and the Consent Form. [Hiroshima]
  • What you sent, from your list of enclosed documents: your VTU transcripts for semesters 1 to 8; your course completion certificate; the research plan; letters from your Layerpath CEO (Layerpath) and Dr Smitha J A (Head of CSE), plus an extra letter from Prof. Sadhana R; your IELTS result and the certificate that your degree was taught in English; Personal History; the form about yourself; and the Consent Form completed by Prof. Zheng.
  • The form about yourself exists for this meeting, so the professors will probably have it open while they talk to you. [my inference] Yours covers your reason for choosing Hiroshima, hackathons and side projects, the 2026 studies that "impressed you recently", hobbies and skills, your jobs, and the 2023 paper and award. Your copy is 04_My_Proposal/My_Submitted_Form.md. In one report on Hiroshima's third-year transfer route into informatics (B8 in file 02), the professors asked about what the applicant wrote on this kind of form. So expect a question on anything you wrote there. [my inference, from file 02]

Before the day

  • Your application is complete. [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Your applicant number on UCARO, and the card that shows it, appear after 12:00 JST on 19 Nov 2026 (08:30 IST). This date is planned, not fixed. [Hiroshima] and [Office, 12 Nov]
  • Learn your applicant number by heart. In four separate reports from Hiroshima (one on this program, three on the informatics transfer route), the first question reported was "What is your applicant number?" See file 02.

After the meeting

  • Hiroshima's decision is planned for Monday 14 December 2026, by email to accepted applicants only. [Hiroshima]

What we don't know yet

  • The exact date and time.
  • Teams or Zoom. (Hiroshima gives Teams and Zoom only as examples. Prof. Zheng used Teams on 2 Nov.)
  • Which professors will join, and how many. Prof. Zheng will probably be there, but that is not confirmed. Prof. Dohi and Prof. Okamura are possible, but nothing says so.
  • Whether you may share slides on screen, and whether they want the slides sent beforehand.
  • What "your research" means for the first 10 minutes: your graduation project, your 2023 review paper, or your work with coding agents at Layerpath.
  • Whether there will be maths or basic informatics questions, and in what form (asked aloud, or a problem shown on screen).
  • Whether you may keep a printed copy of your research plan with you.
  • Whether there is a connection check before the day, and what happens if your connection drops.
  • The language. Everything so far has been in English, but nothing says the meeting must be.

Questions to ask Prof. Zheng when his scheduling email arrives

Keep it to these three. They are short and he can answer each in a line.

  1. The split. "Is it about 10 minutes on my past work and 5 minutes on my research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions? For the past work, should I talk about my graduation project, or my work with AI coding agents at Layerpath?"
  2. Platform and slides. "Will it be on Teams or Zoom, and may I share slides on screen? Should I send them before the meeting?"
  3. Materials. "May I keep a printed copy of my research plan with me during the questions? Is there anything else I should prepare?"

(Don't ask which professors will join. He may not be able to say in advance, and asking can sound like you want an advantage. [my inference])

If he seems happy to say more, one optional fourth: "Should I expect questions on basic mathematics, such as probability or linear algebra?"

A draft you can adapt (check 12_Meeting_Day/04_email_templates.md for the tone the kit uses):

Dear Prof. Zheng,

Thank you for the schedule. I have three short questions so I can prepare well.

  1. Is the format about 10 minutes on my past work and 5 minutes on my research plan, then about 15 minutes of questions? For the past work, should I focus on my graduation project or on my work with AI coding agents?
  2. Will the meeting be on Teams or Zoom, and may I share slides on screen?
  3. May I keep a printed copy of my research plan with me during the questions? Is there anything else I should prepare?

Best regards, Sudhanshu Pandit

How this compares with other programs (context only)

  • In the regular admission route for the same program, applicants first answer a written question set, then meet the professors the next day (in the August 2025 round: written questions on 28 Aug, the meeting on 29 Aug). Source: Hiroshima, regular admission, August 2025 round (PDF).
  • In your route there is no written question set, so the meeting is the only place the professors can hear what you know in your field. [my inference] That is a reason to be ready for a few basic maths or informatics questions, even though Prof. Zheng expects most questions to be on your plan. The question sets in 03_Previous_Years_Questions/ show what this faculty treats as basic knowledge.
  • Similar online meetings elsewhere: the University of Tokyo's international admission route (Earth and Planetary Science) uses a 10-minute presentation of research background and plan plus 10 minutes of questions on Zoom. NAIST Information Science uses a 20-minute online meeting with maths questions asked aloud, plus questions on the research proposal. Details and links are in file 02.
