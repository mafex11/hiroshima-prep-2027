Reading list before the meeting

When and how long: Spread over the days before the meeting.

Total: about 20 hours for everything below, plus about 2 hours of optional extras. Part 1, your plan's references, is about 10.5 hours. Part 2, the lab's other papers, is about 9 hours. Part 3 is 30 minutes. Times are rough.

How to use it:

  • Most important first. If time runs short, do Part 1, then 2A, then every "abstract only" entry (31 of them, about 1 hour in all), then the longer reads.
  • Say a line only after you have read the parts it names. If you read less, name only what you read. "I've read the abstract" is a good answer.
  • PDFs are in 07_Lab_Papers/pdfs/. Abstract files are in ~/code/personal/masters-research/2027-refresh/hiroshima-application/qbank-2026-11-01/paper_text/, written paper_text/ below. "Paywalled" means the publisher charges for the full text and no copy is on your Mac.
  • The "read in full" marks in the kit's paper guides show what the research agents read, not what you read.

  • Then say: "I've read the abstract; the full text is behind the Springer paywall. Could I ask you for a copy?"

[2] Guo, Okamura and Dohi, "Optimal test case generation for boundary value analysis" (Software Quality Journal 32(2), 2024)

  • PDF: REF2_Guo_Okamura_Dohi_2024_SQJ_boundary_value_BCD.pdf. Journal page first, PDF page in brackets.
  • Read: the abstract, p.543 [1]; Section 2 with Remarks 1 and 2, pp.546-548 [4-6]; Section 3, the BCD definition with its lower and upper limits, pp.548-550 [6-8]; Section 5.6 with Tables 5 and 6, pp.560-561 [18-19]; the limits in Section 6, Conclusion, pp.562-563 [20-21].
  • Optional: the introduction, pp.543-546 [1-4].
  • Time: about 45 minutes; about 1.5 hours with the introduction at a slow pace.
  • Then say: "I've read the abstract, the BCD definition with Remarks 1 and 2, and the results in Section 5.6."
  • Watch out: the mean-vs-max line is on p.560 ("A1 with BCDmean accepts more candidates than A1 with BCDmax, as shown in Table 6"). But Table 6 also shows A3 accepting far more (0.07 to 0.42) while killing fewer mutants, so acceptance alone does not explain the results.
  • Guo's later boundary value papers (2023 and 2025) are in Part 2B.

[3] Qiu, Dohi, Wu, Zheng and Okamura, neural-kernel self-exciting model (IJMEMS 11(5), 2026)

  • PDF: REF3_Qiu_et_al_2026_IJMEMS_Hawkes_SRM.pdf.
  • Read: the abstract (PDF p.1, journal p.1907); Section 2 (PDF pp.4-9, journal pp.1910-1915); Section 3 with 3.2, the neural kernel (PDF pp.9-11, journal pp.1915-1917); Section 5, the experiments (PDF pp.14-19, journal pp.1920-1925), including 5.4 with Tables 4 and 5.
  • Time: about 90 minutes. Short version: the abstract, 3.2 and 5.4 only, about 40 minutes.
  • Then say: "I read the abstract, sections 2 and 3, and the experiments in section 5. Against ten Hawkes models with fixed kernels, the neural kernel had the best PMSE on five of the eight datasets at both the 20% and the 50% points, and at 50% it beat the NHPP models on seven of eight. At 80% it was best on only two, and the paper says it may overfit a little there."
  • Watch out: keep "5 of 8" (against the ten Hawkes kernels, Table 4) apart from "7 of 8" (against the best NHPP model per dataset at 50%, Table 5; DS7 is the one miss, 4.209 vs 2.156).
  • The other Hawkes papers from the same group are in Part 2C.

  • Time: about 40 minutes in all.
  • Then say: "I've read the pysrat README, including how it takes data and the note that an offset turns the cloglog model into a Cox model, and the metrics-models page."
  • After you run it yourself: "I ran pysrat 0.2.8: the README quick start, then all 12 models on the Musa SS1A data that comes with it, and the log-logistic model fitted best by AIC."
  • Watch out: pysrat was installed for you on 14 Nov and the example was run for you then (the plots are from that run), not by you. Don't say "I installed it" unless you run pip install pysrat yourself, and say "I ran it" only after you run the script.
