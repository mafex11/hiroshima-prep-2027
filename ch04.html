Questions professors asked other applicants

When and how long: Fri 13 Nov, 30 minutes.

Searched 12 Nov 2026, in English, Japanese and Chinese.

How much of this is about your meeting

Be clear about this before you read on.

  • No first-hand report of a meeting like yours was found. Yours is the Informatics and Data Science Program, admission route for international students living outside Japan, an online meeting with the professors. Nobody seems to have written about it in English, Japanese or Chinese.
  • The closest real material, in order of how close it is:
    1. Prof. Zheng's own description, plus the questions he asked you on 2 Nov (source A1).
    2. One report on the same program's regular admission route meeting with the professors, January 2026 (source A2). In that route the meeting comes after a set of written questions, so it is short.
    3. Eight reports on the third-year transfer route into Hiroshima's School of Informatics and Data Science (情報科学部), 2019 to 2026 (sources B1 to B8). This is an undergraduate route, not yours. But the professors asking are Hiroshima informatics teachers, and the reports show how they question people. The School shares its support office (工学系総括支援室) with your program, according to the contact list on the page where Hiroshima posts its previous years' questions. I could not confirm whether the same professors will be in your meeting.
    4. Similar online meetings at other Japanese universities (sources C1 to C4).
  • The regular admission route's written question sets are real and official, but they are written, not spoken. Our versions are in 03_Previous_Years_Questions/.

Where I looked and found nothing useful: Zhihu (知乎) and Chinese agency sites (新东方, 蔚蓝, 荞麦, 引路, 芥末, 育宸); Xiaohongshu (小红书) does not show up in web search; CSDN (only question lists for Chinese domestic programs); Japanese note.com and Hatena blogs; Reddit; YouTube; Hiroshima University's own pages of student stories. A. Hiroshima, Informatics and Data Science Program

A1. Prof. Junjun Zheng, Teams call, 2 Nov 2026

Source: the 2 Nov call, summarised in zheng_meeting_2_nov_review.md in this folder. The quotes come from speech-to-text, so wording is rough.

What he said the meeting will be like: about 10 minutes on your research, about 5 on your proposal, about 15 minutes of questions. Questions "maybe not limited to research proposal", and they depend on which professors join. Likely: "Why did you choose Japan?" and "Why did you choose Hiroshima University?". "Most of QA will focus on the research proposal."

What he actually asked you in that call (a real talk with your prospective supervisor, not the other professors):

  • "Why are you thinking about going back to university for a master degree?" (13:32:40)
  • "Why Japan?" (13:33:23, transcribed as "And the wild Japan.")
  • "Can you speak a little Japanese?" (13:34:20)
  • "Do you have a plan to learn Japanese?" (13:34:33)
  • "What got you interested in this topic?" (13:35:51)
  • On testing AI-written code: "But actually, is it very difficult?" (13:38:21). This may have been a remark ("it is very difficult") rather than a question, but you answered it.
  • "Do you know our lab?" (13:42:22)
  • "Do you have any questions about the research topic?" (13:46:46)
  • "You published a paper, right?" (13:54:26)

A2. Zhihu post by 令珂 about this program's January 2026 admissions round (winter entry; its title gives the date 2026.2)

  • URL: https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2010825973415818069
  • Last edited 8 Nov 2026 (「编辑于 2026-11-08」).
  • What it is: a Chinese applicant, moving from humanities to science, who got into this program through the regular admission route in January 2026 (winter entry). Their description of the written questions matches the January 2026 question set (03_Previous_Years_Questions/2026_January.md: a probability question built on the gamma function, a memoryless information source, a heap question, a circular queue in C), so it is genuine about this program.
  • On the meeting with the professors, in full (translated from Chinese): "It was just a formality. They asked my applicant number, what research I wanted to do, and whether I would go on in research or join a company afterwards. Three or four questions and it was over."
  • In the comments, a reader asked how to answer "research or a company". The writer replied that they had said a company, and felt either answer was fine.
  • Also: they contacted more than ten professors, and only the Hiroshima professor replied that they would take them once they got through the admissions round.
  • Why it matters: in the regular admission route the written questions carry the weight, so the meeting is short. In your route there are no written questions, so expect a longer and deeper meeting. But the questions they chose (your research, research or industry) are a fair guide to what they care about.

B. Hiroshima School of Informatics and Data Science (情報科学部), third-year transfer route

Undergraduate transfer route for technical-college (高専) students. The meetings are in Japanese, in person, about 6 to 20 minutes, almost always with two professors. Listed oldest first.

B1. 槁木死灰, a Hatena blog post on transferring into Hiroshima's School of Informatics (round held in 2019)

  • URL: https://tuktuk-bosi.hatenablog.com/entry/2019/10/17/134518 (posted 17 Oct 2019)
  • 10 to 15 minutes each, two professors per room.
  • Questions:
    • 志望動機は？ (Why do you want to come here?)
    • Knowledge question 1: データの平均値を計算したいとき、どういうプログラムを作りますか？ (If you want to compute the mean of some data, what program would you write?) The writer explained arrays and a for loop in C and got "まあいいでしょう" ("that will do"). Any language seemed fine if you explain the algorithm and the code.
    • Knowledge question 2: 相関係数について教えてください (Tell me about the correlation coefficient.) The writer could not answer properly and said the professors saw through it.

B2. しんちゃん, a Hatena blog post on transferring into Hiroshima's School of Informatics (round held in 2021)

  • URL: https://shinchankosen.hatenadiary.jp/entry/2021/08/23/121449 (23 Aug 2021)
  • Two professors, under 10 minutes. 「去年まで分散とか相関係数とかについて聞かれる感じだったのに、機械学習についてたくさん聞かれました」 ("Until last year they asked about variance and the correlation coefficient. This year it was lots of machine learning.")
  • Questions:
    • Applicant number and name.
    • 志望動機 (Why here?)
    • 競技プログラミングどんな感じでやってきたの？ (How have you done competitive programming?), because the writer mentioned it.
    • 機械学習と統計解析の違いはなにか (What is the difference between machine learning and statistical analysis?)
    • その統計的知識って？ (What do you mean by "statistical knowledge"?)
    • 区間って言いましたけど他には？ (You said "intervals". What else?) They wanted "estimation and testing".
    • 多次元のときは？ (What about many dimensions?)
    • 機械学習って現実でどんな風に使われてる？ (How is machine learning used in real life?)
    • じゃあそのペッパーって入力と出力どうなってる？ (Then what are the inputs and outputs of that Pepper robot?)
    • 機械学習の実現方法1つ挙げて説明してください (Name one way to implement machine learning and explain it.)

B3. Takoのブログ, a Hatena blog post on the transfer round into Hiroshima's School of Informatics for the 2022 school year

  • URL: https://tako0218.hatenablog.com/entry/2021/09/01/164537 (1 Sep 2021, same admissions year as B2, a different person)
  • About 10 minutes each, two rooms. Applicants compared notes and 「質問は非常に似通っていました」 ("the questions were very similar").
  • Order: 志望動機 → 高専での研究内容 → 広島大学で何をしたいか → データ統計と機械学習の違い (Why here, then your research at college, then what you want to do at Hiroshima, then the difference between data statistics and machine learning). Most people felt unsure about the last one.

B8. 豊原備忘録, a Hatena blog post on getting into Hiroshima's School of Informatics as a third-year transfer, 2027 school year

  • URL: https://basyoengine.hatenablog.com/entry/2026/07/10/230530 (10 Jul 2026). Recommendation route.
  • Two professors, 20 minutes:
    1. 志望理由（2分） (Reason for applying, 2 minutes.)
    2. 大学入ってしたいこと，将来何するか (What you want to do here, and in the future.)
    3. 卒研について (Your graduation research.) A professor asked from what the writer had written on the application form (調書), with two or three follow-ups.
    4. Knowledge questions answered aloud (8 minutes): choose two of three:
      • f(x) が x=a において連続の定義 (Define continuity of f at x = a.)
      • 線型空間の基底の定義 (Define a basis of a vector space.)

  • The writer had expected questions on basic informatics, but it was all maths: 「全然数学の問題だった」 ("it was all maths problems").

C. Similar meetings at other Japanese universities

These are not Hiroshima. They show how other Japanese graduate programs run their meetings with applicants, so the list below is grounded even where Hiroshima reports are thin. C1 is earth and planetary science, not informatics. C1 and C2 are online; the Tsukuba page (C4) does not say whether its master's meeting is online.

C1. University of Tokyo, Earth and Planetary Science, international admission route (official)

  • URL: https://www.eps.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/graduateadmission_en/international_applicants_en/
  • Master's: a 20-minute meeting. It is a 10-minute presentation of your research background and your research plan at the University of Tokyo, then 10 minutes of questions from the professors. It is held online on Zoom. Close to your format.

C2. NAIST, 2027 Master's guide (official)

  • URL: https://www.naist.jp/en/international_students/prospective_students/admission_information/file/2027_Master's_course.pdf (section 7, on how applicants are selected)
  • A 20-minute meeting "in Japanese or English", "conducted remotely using an online conferencing system".
  • Information Science: (i) maths questions answered aloud (algebra, analysis, etc.); (ii) questions on the research proposal and on areas of information science relevant to the applicant's area of specialization. Algebra scope: Strang, Introduction to Linear Algebra, 4th ed., chapters 1 to 7. Analysis: Lang, A First Course in Calculus, 5th ed., chapters 1 to 15.

C3. NAIST Information Science, two applicant reports (note.com)

  • Umi, https://note.com/tender_kalmia455/n/n1dc7d33e06b8 (8 Dec 2025, spring 2026 entry). Professors: the professor of the lab they wanted plus two others. Maths: a short projection problem and drawing a hyperbola. Questions:
    • お決まりの研究概要説明（3分くらい） (The usual research summary, about 3 minutes.)
    • 小論文で書いた専門用語の説明を求められる (Explain technical terms you used in your essay.)
    • 小論文に書いた条件について根拠を求められる (Justify the conditions you set.)
    • 小論文に書いた条件を拡張した場合について聞かれる (What if those conditions were extended?)
    • 研究の前提が崩れる可能性を指摘される (They pointed out that a premise of the study might fail.)
    • 使う予定の解析モデルの仕組みを知っているか聞かれる (Do you know how the model you plan to use works?) The writer could not answer this one.
    • Tip from the writer: when asked why method A, they asked "I wrote A, but after more reading I think B is better. May I explain B?" and the professors agreed.
  • 卯の花, https://note.com/one_uno_hana/n/nd4ef1e59ffdd (14 Dec 2023). Maths: integrate log x / x from e to e², and diagonalise a 3×3 matrix. Questions:
    • 3分で小論文の内容を説明して (Explain your essay in 3 minutes.) 「頻出っぽいので必ず準備しましょう」 ("Seems to come up every time, so prepare it.")
    • その研究の意義 (Why is this research needed?)
    • そのやり方ではなく〜というやり方はある？ (Instead of your method, what about method X?) This was something the professor had mentioned during a lab visit. The writer had not read the paper he suggested.
    • 小論文中の表の意味は？ (What does the table in your essay mean?)
    • なぜ従来手法じゃダメか (Why is the existing method not enough?)
    • 小論文中の言葉の意味 (What does this word in your essay mean?)

C4. University of Tsukuba, Computer Science, master's meeting with professors (official, April 2027 entry)

  • URL: https://www.cs.tsukuba.ac.jp/english/admission.html
  • The meeting has two parts: a general talk ("your current research works, readiness for post-entry research, and purpose of enrollment") and questions on basic knowledge. In the second part you get 15 minutes to work on four posted problems, then 2 minutes to give your answers aloud. You may use notepaper for working, but the answers must be spoken. Areas: analysis, linear algebra, discrete mathematics and logic (including Boolean algebra, Karnaugh maps, combinational circuits), and programming fundamentals (stacks, queues, lists, trees, recursion; C and Python).

What this means for you

  • The one report on this program's meeting with the professors (A2) is short and simple: your number, your research, research or industry. Your meeting will be longer, because in your route there are no written questions before it.
  • Hiroshima informatics teachers like one or two plain basic questions, asked aloud, on statistics, ML, C or the applicant's own topic (B1 to B6). In B8 they were all maths. B7 reported none. Answers did not need to be perfect, but faking was noticed (B1).
  • In both NAIST reports, the questions on a research plan followed the same pattern: summarise it in 3 minutes, explain your terms, justify your assumptions, say why it matters, and say what you would do if a premise fails (C3). Prepare those five for your own plan.
  • Weak opening answers led to more probing questions (B6). A clear 3-minute summary of your plan keeps the talk on ground you know.
