Assoc. Prof. Junjun Zheng: who he is

When and how long: Sat 21 Nov, 30 minutes.

Checked on 29 Oct 2026 and again on 13 Nov 2026. Main source: his Hiroshima University researcher page (last updated 5 Nov 2026): https://seeds.office.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/profile/en.c1b1491e563a3c40520e17560c007669.html Lab member page (links to it): https://www.rel.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/en/members/ Paper details below come from Crossref, OpenAlex and the Springer page. Where I only saw a title or an abstract, I say so.

Warning: OpenAlex mixes him up with a different Junjun Zheng at Alibaba (RecGPT, ShopX and so on). Those papers are not his.

How you got here: on 29 Oct Prof. Dohi replied that your topic fits Prof. Zheng rather than him, and that he will retire in a few years (his words: "I will retire after a few years"). Prof. Zheng wrote the same day that he would be happy to supervise your research, with Prof. Okamura and Prof. Dohi in copy. After your call on 2 Nov he emailed you the signed Consent Form and asked for your transcript and IELTS report. So treat Prof. Zheng as your supervisor, and don't ask Prof. Dohi to be involved.

One small human point: he came from China to Hiroshima for his master's and doctorate, so he knows what it is like to start in Japan as an international student. Do not bring it up yourself. It is just good to know.

What he works on, in plain words

  1. Availability and software rejuvenation. Software slowly gets worse as it runs (memory leaks and so on). This is called aging. Rejuvenation means restarting it on purpose before it fails. Checkpointing means saving its state so you lose less when it does fail. He builds probability models of this and finds the restart timing that keeps the system up the most. Many papers, mostly from 2012 to 2022 plus book chapters in 2024 and 2026, for example in IEEE Transactions on Reliability (2017), Reliability Engineering and System Safety (2018) and Software Quality Journal (2020).
  2. Markov regenerative processes (MRGP) and non-Markovian models. These are the maths behind point 1. A plain Markov model assumes every wait is "memoryless" (exponential). A fixed restart timer is not memoryless, so he needs MRGPs or "phase expansion", a trick that turns a non-exponential wait into a chain of small exponential steps. He also studies sensitivity: how much the answer (for example availability) moves when a parameter changes. Examples: ISSREW 2021, Stochastic Models 2022, Performance Evaluation 2024.
  3. Uncertainty in model answers. If the model's input numbers are only estimates, how unsure is the output? Several papers on uncertainty propagation, including "epistemic uncertainty propagation" (2019 to 2025).
  4. Software reliability models. NHPP models, Hawkes (self-exciting) models, kernel regression, bootstrap, boosting, metrics-based models. Mostly with Prof. Dohi, Dr. Wu and doctoral students. One of these is in your proposal: Qiu, Dohi, Wu, Zheng, Okamura (IJMEMS 2026), your reference [3].
  5. Security and networks. Intrusion-tolerant systems, malware classification with neural networks, and handover in mixed (heterogeneous) mobile networks modelled with stochastic Petri nets (SRSE 2024, Mathematics 2025). The INFOCOM 2026 paper (Zhu et al., he is a co-author) proposes a 5G handover rule and tests it by simulation, not with Petri nets.
  6. LLMs. The N-version programming paper with generative AI (your reference [1], he is first author) and a 2026 multi-agent LLM paper (he is a co-author). The lab's page says they now ask whether generative AI can make N-version programming stronger, and how several agents should work together.

What he will probably care about in the meeting

These are my guesses from his work, not things he said, unless marked "2 Nov".

  1. Is the probability model sound? He builds Markov and non-Markov models for a living. Expect "what exactly is random here?", "what do you assume?", "why a Beta distribution?". Know your beta-binomial cold (see 05_Questions_on_My_Proposal/07_questions_on_my_proposal.md, section 4, Q14 to Q17).
  2. How sure are your numbers? He has papers on sensitivity and on uncertainty in model outputs. Your answer: the bootstrap that resamples runs gives intervals for the correlation and for the vote failure chance, not just one number.
  3. The "best number" question. His rejuvenation papers find the best timing that maximises availability. Your plan finds the number of runs N that minimises cost plus expected failure cost. Same kind of question. You can say that, plainly.
  4. How your study extends his N-version paper. He varied temperature and conversation history reuse. Your plan varies the context file, the agent, the model and the temperature, on real tasks. (2 Nov: he said newer models don't let you set the temperature, which is why they did not compare new models. Your own harness is where you set it.)
  5. Can you do it in two years, and is your maths ready? He teaches linear algebra and decision-making to undergraduates. Be honest that your only statistics course is BCS301.
  6. The application itself. Already done: he signed the Consent Form and sent it to you on 2 Nov, and asked for your transcript and IELTS report the same day. What is left is the 30-minute meeting with the professors (about 15 minutes of presentation, then about 15 minutes of questions), where he is very likely to be one of the professors.

3 papers to skim before the meeting

1. Can Generative AI Enhance the Effectiveness of N-Version Programming? (read this one first)

J. Zheng, H. Okamura, T. Dohi. SFPVV 2025, LNCS 16356, pp. 1-16, Springer, online 18 Feb 2026. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-6032-5_1

  • They had LLMs write several versions of the same program, then checked how correct and how different the versions were.
  • They compared a strong model (GPT-4o) with weaker ones, and tried two ways to get more variety: changing the temperature and reusing the conversation history.
  • Voting made the strong model's results more reliable but helped little with weak models, and more variety did not always mean more reliability.

Note: this is behind a paywall. Only the abstract has been read so far (abstract rechecked 13 Nov). Do not quote any number from it. On 2 Nov he said they used GPT-4o and GPT-4o mini (the speech-to-text record is rough at that point) and that they varied the temperature. It is fine to ask him for a copy by email.

2. Availability Analysis of Software Systems with Rejuvenation and Checkpointing

J. Zheng, H. Okamura, T. Dohi. Mathematics 9(8), 846, 2021. Open access. https://doi.org/10.3390/math9080846

  • A system that ages gets restarted on a timer (rejuvenation) and saves its state (checkpointing), and the operator can make mistakes while checkpointing.
  • They model it with a stochastic Petri reward net. The result is not a standard semi-Markov or Markov regenerative model, so they solve it with phase expansion.
  • They find the restart timing that gives the highest long-run availability, and show that operator mistakes lower availability and clearly shift the best timing.

3. Toward Scalable LLM-Based Multi-Agent Collaboration: A Dynamic Task Graph Approach with Asynchronous Parallel Execution

J. Yu, Y. Ding, J. Dai, J. Zheng, J. Wu, H. Sato. Electronics 15(11), 2475, 2026. Open access. Zheng is 4th of 6 authors; Yu and Ding contributed equally and Ding is the corresponding author. It extends their ICAPS 2025 paper. PDF: 07_Lab_Papers/pdfs/LAB5_Yu_et_al_2026_Electronics_DynTaskMAS.pdf. https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15112475

  • DynTaskMAS lets many LLM agents work in parallel by keeping a task graph that is updated while the work runs, instead of a fixed step-by-step pipeline.
  • It has four parts: a task splitter, a scheduler that hands ready tasks to free agents, a shared-context layer, and a controller that adjusts priorities to the load.
  • Against step-by-step baselines it cut execution time by 21.3 to 33.0% (21.3% for simple tasks, 33.0% for complex ones), raised GPU use from 65% to 88%, and gave 3.47 times the throughput with 16 agents.

Optional 4th, if you have time: On the sensitivity of stationary solutions of Markov regenerative processes

J. Zheng, H. Okamura, T. Dohi. Performance Evaluation 164, 102397, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.peva.2024.102397 (preprint: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4459340) I could not open the abstract (ScienceDirect and SSRN both refused automated access, last tried 13 Nov), so I only know the title. From the title: it asks how much the long-run behaviour of an MRGP changes when its parameters change. Skim the preprint's first page before you mention it at all.

Do not say

  • That you read the full N-version paper (unless you get it and read it before the meeting).
  • Anything about his rejuvenation or MRGP work beyond what is written above.
  • That the lab works on coding agents or context files. It does not, as far as we found. That part is what you bring.
