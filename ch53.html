Result in one line

I found no paper by Prof. Zheng, Prof. Dohi or Prof. Okamura published or posted between 1 Oct and 12 Nov 2026 that is not already in GUIDE_2. One paper from late September is missing from GUIDE_2, so it is summarised below. Nothing new was open access, so I did not create the new_since_Oct_2026/ folder.

1. The one paper missing from GUIDE_2 (online 26 Sep 2026)

Age replacement policies with random intervals for parallel systems

J. Wu, L. Lin, X. Zhao, T. Dohi. Reliability Engineering and System Safety 278, 113564. Online 26 Sep 2026 (Crossref record date); the volume is dated Feb 2027. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ress.2026.113564

  • Access: paywalled. OpenAlex says "closed" and Semantic Scholar says "CLOSED". Not downloaded.
  • Authors' places (OpenAlex): Jing Wu, Huai'an University; Liqu Lin and Xufeng Zhao, Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics; Tadashi Dohi, Hiroshima University. Jing Wu is not Dr. Jingchi Wu of the lab.
  • Why it was missed: GUIDE_2 lists only a selection of Prof. Dohi's papers without Prof. Zheng. The lab's own website now shows this paper as its newest publication (https://www.rel.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/en/).

What it does (from the abstract only, read on the publisher page through the DOI on 12 Nov; on 13 Nov the publisher and the abstract databases refused automated access, so this summary could not be rechecked):

  • In real plants, preventive replacement can't happen at any moment. It happens inside a maintenance window, and the window itself is random because work cycles end at uncertain times.
  • For a parallel system (several redundant units, the system works while at least one does), the system is replaced when a work cycle ends inside the window, or at the window's upper bound otherwise.
  • They choose the window bounds and the best number of units by minimising the expected replacement cost per unit time, and study how the window, the redundancy level and the costs change the best policy.
  • They extend it to an uncertain number of units (a Poisson random variable) and illustrate it with pump maintenance in a nuclear power plant's cooling water system, using illustrative data.

Relevance to your proposal: medium. Picking the number of redundant units by cost is the same kind of question as your "odd number of runs N that minimises c_run N + c_fail P(majority of N fails)". The big difference: in a parallel system the units are usually modelled as failing independently, while your whole point is that agent runs share failures. One line you could say, if Prof. Dohi brings it up and you have read the abstract yourself first (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ress.2026.113564): "I've read only the abstract. It also picks the best number of units by cost, so it's the same kind of trade-off as my best N, but with dependent runs."

Do not quote any number from it. You have not read the full paper.
