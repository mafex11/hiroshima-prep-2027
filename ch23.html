Chapter 23 of 28

Oral exam prep (online, 24 Nov to 2 Dec 2026)

When and how long: From 3 Nov, a little every day.

What the guideline says: "Your academic abilities regarding knowledge in the specialized field, application capability, and problem-solving abilities will be evaluated by the oral examination via internet using remote conferencing systems such as Teams or Zoom. Details of the oral examination will be notified on the day of the entrance examination." The oral exam and your documents are each scored A, B or C.

6. Likely oral exam topics for this lab (revise before 24 Nov)

The exam tests "knowledge in the specialized field" (NOTES.md). With this lab, expect probability and statistics.

  1. NHPP basics. N(t) counts faults found by time t; mean value function Λ(t) = ω F(t), where ω is the expected total number of faults and F a fault-detection time distribution. Goel-Okumoto: Λ(t) = ω(1 − e^(−bt)). Software reliability over (t, t + x]: R(x | t) = exp(−[Λ(t + x) − Λ(t)]). Expected remaining faults at t: ω − Λ(t).
  2. Maximum likelihood for time data. log L = Σ log λ(t_i) − Λ(T) for event times t_1..t_n up to T. Why EM: treat the unobserved total fault count as missing data; global convergence (P5).
  3. Bootstrap. Parametric bootstrap: simulate fault data from the fitted model, refit, take percentiles for intervals. Useful when counts are small, as in his field data.
  4. Hawkes process. λ(t | H_t) = μ(t) + Σ_{t_i < t} φ(t − t_i). Self-excitation; cluster view; reduces to NHPP if φ ignores past events (P3, Eq. 9 and 10).
  5. N-version programming. With independent versions and failure probability p each, a 2-out-of-3 vote fails with probability 3p²(1 − p) + p³. Knight and Leveson: expected coincident failures under independence μ = T·P_m with P_m = 1 − P_0 − P_1; z = (K − μ)/sqrt(T·P_m(1 − P_m)) (R1, Eq. 1 to 4). Eckhardt and Lee: independence cannot be assumed because inputs differ in difficulty.
  6. Boundary value analysis. Equivalence partitions, off-by-one faults, mutation operators (ROR, AOR, SVR, LOR), kill rate (P2).
  7. His own design (v2). Beta-binomial: if k runs on input x fail with chance theta(x) and theta(X) ~ Beta(a, b), then P(two fail) = E[theta^2] = p^2 + Var theta, the intraclass correlation is rho = 1/(a + b + 1), and a 2-out-of-3 vote fails with E[3 theta^2 - 2 theta^3]. Why the bootstrap resamples runs (the same runs meet every input). Why most inputs may have zero failures (be ready to discuss a zero-inflated variant). Why 10 runs per setting. The cost trade-off for N. The NHPP with arrivals m(t) = sum_j omega_j G(t - tau_j) and why a finite-fault model fails when code keeps arriving.

How to answer on Teams

  • Answer short first, then add one example. Stop and let them ask more.
  • If you don't know: "I don't know that yet. I would start by ..."
  • Think out loud on problems. Say each step. Write on paper and hold it up if they allow it.
  • Have a pen, paper and the proposal PDF nearby.
