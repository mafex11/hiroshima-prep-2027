Part 1: Before Monday's meeting (30 Oct to 2 Nov)

Chapter 15 of 28

Questions to ask Prof. Zheng

When and how long: Sunday, 15 minutes.

What this file is: the questions for Monday's call, in the order to ask them, with the exact words, why each one matters, and what to listen for. It expands the earlier list in /Users/mafex/code/personal/masters-research/2027-refresh/hiroshima-application/meeting-prep/ASK_HIM.md.

How to use it:

  • Questions 1 to 4 are must-ask. The 13 Nov arrival deadline depends on them. Ask them even if the research talk runs long. If it is 13:50 IST and you have not started them, start them then.
  • Questions 5 to 12 only if there is time. Pick two at most.
  • If he already answered something, do not ask it again. Say: "You already answered my question about the oral exam. Thank you."
  • Write his answers on paper during the call. Copy them into the notes block at the end of this file the same day.
  • Listen, say thank you, write it down. Do not argue with an answer, even if it means more work.

Must-ask (1 to 4)

1. The Consent Form (most important)

"Thank you again for agreeing to supervise me. Could you sign the Consent Form for my application? I can send it to you today with my name filled in. The guideline says all documents go together, and I plan to courier everything from India on 6 November. Would it be easier for you to give the signed original to the Support Office directly, or should I put it in my envelope?"

Then:

"Is there anything you'd like me to know for the 'Details of Consent' box? I understand you fill that part."

  • Why: document 8 is a Consent Form signed by the prospective supervisor. Everything must arrive by Fri 13 Nov, 17:00 JST. Post from Japan to India and back is too slow, so the original may be best going from him straight to the Support Office, which is on the same Higashi-Hiroshima campus. Only he and the office can say which way is accepted.
  • Listen for: "I'll hand it to the office" or "I'll send you a scan". A date. Whether he wants a PDF or the Word file.
  • If he is not sure: "Then I'll ask the Support Office and copy you, if that's all right." (Email 5 in 04_email_templates.md.)
  • What he fills, not you: the date, "Program (所属)", "Prospective Academic Advisor Name (受入教員名)", and "Details of Consent (同意に至った経緯)". You filled only your name: SUDHANSHU PANDIT.
  • File: /Users/mafex/Downloads/Hiroshima-University/05_Application/forms/5_Consent_Form_Sudhanshu_Pandit.docx

2. The research plan on the university form

"Could you look at my research plan on the university form before I send it? I could send it to you by [Tue 3 Nov or Wed 4 Nov]. Is there anything you'd like me to change, add or cut, for example the field data part?"

Then, if he seems open:

"The agent runs would cost about 420 to 590 US dollars. Should I plan on a lab budget for that, or plan to use the open-weight model for more of the runs?"

  • Why: the form itself says to "make the research plan under his or her supervision". He may want changes before it goes. The budget question shows you thought about cost and do not assume the lab will pay.
  • Listen for: sections he wants shorter, a different title, anything to add. Whether the lab has GPUs for an open-weight model.
  • Also confirm: the supervisor line reads "Junjun ZHENG (Associate Professor, Dependable Systems Laboratory)". Ask if anyone else should be named.
  • Watch the date: you need his reply by Thu 5 Nov to print and courier on Fri 6 Nov. Tue 3 Nov is Culture Day, a holiday in Japan, so he may not reply that day.
  • File: /Users/mafex/Downloads/Hiroshima-University/05_Application/forms/1_Research_Plan_Sudhanshu_Pandit.docx

3. The supervisor choice on UCARO

"On the online application I'll list you as my first-choice supervisor. The second and third choices are optional. Would you like me to list Prof. Okamura, or anyone else, or leave them empty?"

  • Why: UCARO step 3 asks for the prospective supervisor from the official list, 1st choice required, 2nd and 3rd optional. Prof. Zheng is on the Informatics and Data Science Program list (Dependable Systems, 1 Sep 2026) with Prof. Dohi and Prof. Okamura. A small question, but it avoids a surprise.
  • Listen for: a name, or "just me".

4. The online oral exam

"What is the oral exam usually like? How long is it, who is on the panel, should I present my plan with slides, and will there be questions on probability and statistics? What should I revise most?"

  • Why: the guideline only says it is online (Teams or Zoom), on one day between 24 Nov and 2 Dec, and that it tests "academic abilities regarding knowledge in the specialized field, application capability, and problem-solving abilities". Details come on the exam day.
  • Listen for: length, slides yes or no, topics (for example NHPP models, maximum likelihood, Markov chains, basic probability), whether he will be on the panel.
  • He may say he cannot tell you much. That is fine. Say: "I understand. Thank you."
  • Your prep for it: 06_Reliability_and_Stats/02_oral_exam_prep.md.

If there is time (5 to 12)

5. What to read before April, and his paper

"What should I read or learn before April? For example a book on stochastic models or reliability, pysrat, or Julia? And could I ask for a copy of your N-version programming paper? I could only read the abstract."

  • Why: shows you will prepare. The paper is behind the Springer paywall, so asking the author is normal.
  • Listen for: book titles, a paper, a tool. Read the first one within a week, before the oral exam.

6. One research question about his study

"In your N-version study you varied temperature and conversation history reuse. My own harness controls the whole prompt, so I could add history reuse as another setting. Do you think it's worth adding?"

  • Why: it engages with his own work, at the level you actually know it (the abstract). It also shows you can take his advice into the plan.
  • Only ask if the research talk has gone well and there are at least 5 minutes left.
  • Listen for: yes, no, or a better setting to vary. Do not promise anything on the spot. "Thank you, I'll think about how to fit that in."

7. Copying him with the Support Office

"The Support Office already knows you agreed, and they said they will check my documents by email before I post them. Should I copy you when I write to them?"

  • Listen for: yes or no. Then do exactly that.

8. Lab life and meetings

"How does the lab work day to day? How often are seminars and one-to-one meetings? Would Prof. Okamura also be involved in my research?"

  • Don't ask about Prof. Dohi's role. He passed you on and will retire in a few years.

9. English in the lab

"Are seminars and lab meetings in English or Japanese? Are there other international students in the lab?"

  • Why: you are a beginner in Japanese. Good to know how much you need by April.

10. Housing

"Is there housing for new international students, like a university dormitory, and when should I apply? Do most students live near the Higashi-Hiroshima campus?"

11. Tuition exemption

"I'm funding my studies myself. Can privately funded international master's students apply for a tuition exemption, and when does that happen?"

  • Tone: you are asking for information, not asking him for money. If he says "ask the student office", that is a full answer.

12. Anything else he wants

"Is there anything else you'd like from me, for example my transcript?"

  • Good to close the question time with this one.

Do not ask

Do not ask Why
"Will I pass?" or "Is my admission sure now?" He cannot answer. The oral exam and documents decide it.
Anything about Prof. Dohi retiring Rude, and not your business.
"Can the lab pay for my studies?" You are self-funded. Never hint at it.
Dates and fees that the guideline already gives Shows you did not read it. They are in 05_Application/01_application_process_and_dates.md.
"Can I start in October instead?" This route is April 2027 only.
Questions about NAIST, or what he thinks of NAIST If NAIST comes up, he asks, you answer honestly. See 01_30min_meeting_game_plan.md, section 7.
