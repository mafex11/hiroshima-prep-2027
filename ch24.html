Questions on how you will fund your studies

When and how long: Fri 20 Nov, 15 minutes. Only answer if they ask.

This file covers money: how you will pay, proof of funds for the visa, the full two-year cost, MEXT and other scholarships, part-time work, the exchange rate, and who pays for the experiment. Official numbers come from the pages listed under Sources at the end. Your own answers are now written in; the PhD funding line in Q3 is a draft for you to check. Parts marked [FILL] are still yours to complete or check before you say them; [CHECK] means nobody has verified it yet.

Before the meeting

  • Don't bring up money yourself. If a professor asks, answer in two to four short sentences and stop.
  • Never ask the lab to pay for your studies or living costs. The only cost question is the API one already in your proposal.
  • Keep the same line as your email of 29 Oct: "I plan to study as a self-funded student, so my enrolment would not depend on a scholarship." Your submitted form says the same ("I can pay for my master's studies myself") and adds that you will apply for the scholarships open to privately financed international students, such as the JASSO Monbukagakusho Honors Scholarship, and for tuition exemption at Hiroshima University. Keep both parts consistent.
  • Give amounts in yen. Use rupees only if he asks.

How you will pay

Q1. How will you pay for your studies?

I'll pay for my studies myself. I've planned for the enrolment fee of 282,000 yen, two years of tuition at 535,800 yen a year, and my living costs. The money comes from my savings from work and from my family. My plan does not depend on any scholarship, though, as I wrote on my form, I'll still apply for the ones open to privately financed students.

Why he asks: Prof. Dohi told you that students who counted on MEXT later cancelled. Prof. Zheng signed your Consent Form on 2 Nov, after you told him you plan to study self-funded ("it's no problem", he said, adding that the university has many scholarship opportunities you can try). The other professors may still want to know you will really come. If he pushes: "I wrote the same in my email on 29 October. Nothing has changed."

Follow-ups (each builds on your last answer)

  1. Prof: "Two years of tuition assumes you finish on time. What if you need an extra semester?"
    You: "Then I'd pay one more semester's tuition, 267,900 yen, plus six months of living, so about 750,000 to 870,000 yen more. I don't plan for that, but I know the number, and the way to avoid it is to keep to the schedule in my plan."
  2. Prof: "Which part of that schedule is most likely to slip?"
    You: "The first half of year one, because I'd be taking the probability, statistics and reliability courses while building the harness, the checkers and the inputs for the pilot. If the pilot runs late, or shows the runs cost more than I planned, I'd cut the number of tasks then, rather than let the main runs spill into year two."
  3. Prof: "What do you lose if you cut tasks?"
    You: "Mainly precision. I compare the two file versions across tasks, so with fewer tasks that comparison gets a wider interval and a small effect could be missed. I'd use the pilot's numbers to work out the smallest effect the smaller design can still detect, and say that limit plainly in the thesis."
