Hiroshima prep: start here

When and how long: First. 10 minutes.

Where things stand (30 Oct 2026)

  1. 20 Oct: you emailed Prof. Tadashi Dohi (Dependable Systems Laboratory, Hiroshima University) about the April 2027 Special Selection for international students living outside Japan.
  2. 28 Oct: you sent a follow-up with a two-page research proposal built on the lab's work (01_Your_Documents/Research_Proposal_Hiroshima_Sudhanshu_Pandit.pdf).
  3. 29 Oct: Prof. Dohi replied that your topic fits Prof. Zheng better, and that he will retire in a few years. You replied that you are self-funded. Assoc. Prof. Junjun Zheng wrote: "I would be happy to supervise your research at Hiroshima University." The Support Office confirmed that your IELTS e-TRF printout is fine, and you told them Prof. Zheng agreed.
  4. 30 Oct: Prof. Zheng set the meeting.

The meeting

  • Monday 2 November 2026, 17:00 to 17:30 Japan time = 13:30 to 14:00 India time.
  • Microsoft Teams. The link is in Prof. Zheng's email of 30 Oct ("Online meeting (Mr. Sudhanshu Pandit)").
  • Purpose, in his words: "to discuss your research plan and the application process".
  • Prof. Dohi and Prof. Okamura are in copy and may join.
  • Join at 13:25 IST.

Decide before the oral exam

If Hiroshima admits you on 14 Dec, the 282,000 yen enrolment fee is not refundable and may be due before the NAIST exam on 10 Mar 2027. Read 05_Application/04_hiroshima_vs_naist_decision.md.

Golden rules for the call

  • Answer short first. One sentence, one example, then stop.
  • Never bluff. "I don't know that yet. I'd start by ..." beats a wrong answer.
  • Only the abstract of his N-version paper has been read. Say so, and ask him for a copy.
  • Be honest about NAIST if he asks. He is holding a place for you.
  • Never ask the lab for money. You are self-funded.
  • Time zone: Japan = India + 3 hours 30 minutes.
