Questions on how you will fund your studies

When and how long: Sunday, 20 minutes: settle the [CONFIRM] lines. Only answer if he asks.

This file covers money: how you will pay, proof of funds for the visa, the full two-year cost, MEXT and other scholarships, part-time work, the exchange rate, and who pays for the experiment. Official numbers come from the pages listed under Sources at the end. Parts marked [FILL] or [CONFIRM] are yours to complete or check before you say them; [CHECK] means nobody has verified it yet.

Before the call

  • Don't bring up money yourself. If Prof. Zheng asks, answer in two to four short sentences and stop.
  • Never ask the lab to pay for your studies or living costs. The only cost question is the API one already in your proposal.
  • Keep the same line as your email of 29 Oct: "I plan to study as a self-funded student, so my enrolment would not depend on a scholarship."
  • Give amounts in yen. Use rupees only if he asks.

How you will pay

Q1. How will you pay for your studies?

I'll pay for my studies myself. I've planned for the enrolment fee of 282,000 yen, two years of tuition at 535,800 yen a year, and my living costs. [CONFIRM: The money comes from my savings from work and my family's support.] My plan does not depend on any scholarship.

Why he asks: Prof. Dohi told you that students who counted on MEXT later cancelled. Prof. Zheng has said he would be happy to supervise you, and he still has to sign your Consent Form, so he wants to know you will really come. If he pushes: "I wrote the same in my email on 29 October. Nothing has changed."

Q2. Where exactly does the money come from?

[CONFIRM: Part of it is my own savings from my work as an engineer, and my family will cover the rest.] [FILL: if you want, a rough split, for example "about half from my savings".] [CONFIRM: The money for both years is already set aside for this.]

Why he asks: "self-funded" is easy to say. A plain, specific answer shows you have actually planned it. Note for you, not to say: your NAIST answer sheet said savings and family "will cover the first year". For Hiroshima, talk about both years, because Prof. Dohi's worry is admitted students who then cancel their entrance.

Q3. Does your family support this?

[CONFIRM: Yes. My family knows I'm applying to study in Japan, and they support it, with money too if I need it.]

Why he asks: he wants to know there is a backup if something goes wrong. If he pushes ("Who in your family?"): [FILL: one plain sentence, for example "My parents", only if you are comfortable saying it.]

Q4. How much will the two years cost in total?

About 3.3 to 3.8 million yen. The enrolment fee and two years of tuition are about 1.35 million yen, and the university's own estimate for living in Higashi-Hiroshima is 80,000 to 100,000 yen a month. [CONFIRM: I've planned for the higher end.] Flights and the first month's set-up costs come on top.

Why he asks: to see whether you know the real number or only the tuition. If he pushes ("In rupees?"): "At about 0.6 rupees to the yen, roughly 20 to 23 lakh rupees." Note for you: JASSO's Honors Scholarship needs monthly money of no more than 90,000 yen on average (S20). Say "planned for the higher end" only if you accept that.

Q5. Where will you live, and have you checked the costs?

The university's estimate for rent in Higashi-Hiroshima is 20,000 to 50,000 yen a month. [CONFIRM: I'd start with one of the apartments the university recommends to new international students, which are about 20,000 to 30,000 yen a month. I'd also apply for a university dormitory if I'm eligible.]

Facts for you (S3):

  • Recommended apartments for new international students: 20,000 to 30,000 yen a month, unfurnished, no time limit. Contact is the Hiroshima University Co-op.
  • International House: single room 5,900 yen plus 2,500 yen common charge a month. Ikenoue Student Dormitory: 4,700 to 15,000 yen plus 1,200 to 2,000 yen common charge. Both have a 1-year limit, and the table lists "newly enrolled short-term international students, students who will stay less than 1 year" as tenants. [CHECK] whether a two-year master's student can get a room.
  • Sunsquare Higashi-Hiroshima: single 20,000 yen plus 3,500 yen common charge, furnished, 1 to 4 years, open to students enrolled or expected at Hiroshima University.
  • College Court and Dormy (with meals): 58,000 to 72,600 yen a month.
  • Another university page (S2, HUSA) says private apartments start at 40,000 yen a month.

Q6. What about the first month: the flight, a deposit, furniture?

Those come on top of the monthly costs. [CONFIRM: I've kept money aside for them.] [FILL: your own estimate for the flight and set-up, in yen.] For a private apartment there are usually one-time fees like a deposit, so I'll check those before I sign anything.

Facts for you: an older university page lists, for private apartments, a deposit of 1 to 3 months' rent, a move-in fee of 1 month's rent and an agent fee of 1 month's rent (S4). The recommended apartments may differ. [CHECK]

Proof of funds for the visa

Q7. Can you show proof that you have the money?

Yes. For the Certificate of Eligibility, the university asks for a Statement of Financial Support and a bank certificate showing at least 960,000 yen for a stay of more than one year. [CONFIRM: I can get that certificate from my bank.] I can send any other document the university or Immigration asks for.

Why he asks: the university applies for your Certificate of Eligibility for you (S6). If Immigration does not issue it, you cannot come, and his place is wasted. If he pushes: "The Immigration form also asks how I will pay each month, and the supporter's job and yearly income. I'll fill it in honestly." (Item 28 of the application form, S9.)

Q8. Is the money in your own account or your family's? Who signs as the supporter?

[CONFIRM, pick one: "The certificate will be from my own account, and I'll sign as my own supporter." / "It will be from my father's or mother's account, and he or she will sign the Statement of Financial Support."]

Note for you, not to say:

  • The university's form asks for the supporter's name, relationship to you, address, phone, occupation, workplace and yearly income, with a signature (S7). If you sign as your own supporter after leaving Layerpath, you will have no current job or salary to write. [CHECK] with the Global Exchange Group (visa support) how to fill it in.
  • Immigration's own document list for universities it has not marked as a "school with a good record" (適正校, Class I or II) also asks for the original bank balance certificate, papers showing how the money was saved over the past year, and proof of the supporter's job and income for the past year (S8, S10). For Class I or II universities those items are not on the list. [CHECK] which applies to Hiroshima University. Either way, keep a year of bank statements and salary slips ready.

Q9. When will you have these documents ready?

The university asks for them about two months before admission, so around early February 2027 for an April start. [CONFIRM: I'll have the bank certificate and the signed statement ready before then.] I'll also have the enrolment fee ready for the enrolment procedure.

Note for you: the enrolment fee is paid at the enrolment procedure. A sister guideline says April entrants do this in early March. [CONFIRM] the date in the admission guide that comes after 14 Dec.

MEXT and scholarships

Q10. Are you applying for the MEXT scholarship?

Not through Hiroshima. Prof. Dohi explained how competitive MEXT is, and I plan to study here as a self-funded student. My plan does not depend on it.

Why he asks: Prof. Dohi raised it in his email of 29 Oct, and Prof. Zheng was in copy. Note for you: [CONFIRM: Are you applying to Nagoya's MEXT program? If yes, do not give a flat "No".] On 28 Oct your Nagoya application (MEXT Data Science program, October 2027, deadline 24 Nov 2026) was marked ready to send. Its guideline says "Scholarship awardees must not decline their enrollment in this course for any reason" (S28), so if you apply and win it, you would go to Nagoya. Don't argue about MEXT and don't ask how to get it.

Q11. Prof. Dohi said some students cancelled when they did not get MEXT. Why are you different?

I understand the worry. My plan here does not depend on MEXT, so not getting it changes nothing for me. [CONFIRM: The money for both years is ready before I come.]

Q12. Will you cancel your entrance if you get no scholarship at all?

No. I've planned to pay everything myself, so a scholarship result is not a reason to cancel.

If he pushes ("Is there anything that could stop you coming?"): "I'm also applying to NAIST for October 2027. [FILL: any other application, for example Nagoya MEXT, if you are really applying.] If I'm admitted here, I'll tell you my decision quickly and honestly, so you don't hold a place for me that I won't take." Note for you: don't promise "I will definitely come" unless you have decided.

Q13. Will you apply for a tuition fee exemption?

If I'm eligible, yes. New students apply after arriving in April, and results come in late July. It depends on need and grades, so I don't count on it.

Facts for you (S15, S16, S17):

  • Privately financed international students and graduate students can apply. For April 2026 entrants the window was 3 to 16 April 2026. Information for the first semester of AY2027 is due around the end of January 2027.
  • You apply again for each semester. You cannot apply once you have paid, and you cannot apply if you are not living in Japan during that semester.
  • For new master's students, grades mean the average score at your previous university and your entrance exam result. Results for the first semester come in late July, with payment due at the end of August.
  • There is also an enrolment fee exemption, but the guide says "only a few applicants" get it each year.

Q14. If you can pay, why apply for an exemption?

I'd apply only if I meet the rules. The university decides that from income documents, not me. My plan doesn't count on it.

Note for you, not to say: the exemption is for students who find it hard to pay, judged on income documents. Don't describe yourself as "in need" to Prof. Zheng. Say only that you would apply if you are eligible.

Q15. Do you know about JASSO or university scholarships for privately financed students?

Yes. JASSO's Honors Scholarship is 48,000 yen a month. The university also has HU-PASP for new graduate students admitted from outside Japan. Places are few, so I see them as a bonus, not part of my plan.

Facts for you:

  • JASSO has a reservation program for students admitted from abroad: 48,000 yen a month, 12 months for April entrants, applied for only through the university (S21). Hiroshima University's Graduate School of Advanced Science and Engineering was on JASSO's list for spring 2026 (S22). [CHECK] the 2027 list.
  • JASSO's regular Honors Scholarship asks for JLPT N2, or English at CEFR B2 or higher, and your monthly money, not counting fees, must average no more than 90,000 yen (S20). [CHECK] how they count IELTS. If you tell the university you will live on more than 90,000 yen a month, you may not qualify.
  • HU-PASP: full tuition exemption for one year and 50,000 yen a month for one year, for about 11 new graduate students, chosen by the university from students admitted through exams taken outside Japan (S18). [CHECK] whether you must apply or are considered automatically. Ask the Support Office by email, not Prof. Zheng in the call.
  • Private foundations: about 40 of them, around 200 students a year, 20,000 to 230,000 yen a month, only for students already enrolled; some through the support office (university recommendation), some by applying yourself (S18).
  • HU International Student Support Fund: meal cards. The AY2026 window was 29 June to 17 July (S19).

Q16. What if you get none of these?

Then I pay everything myself, as planned. The university's own scholarship page says to prepare your study funds before coming to Japan because places are limited, and I've done that. [CONFIRM]

Part-time work

Q17. Do you plan to work part-time?

[CONFIRM: No, not in the first year. I want to spend that time on courses, the pilot and the main runs.] My funding does not depend on part-time work.

If he pushes: "If I ever work later, I'll get permission first, keep within the limit, and make sure it never takes time from research."

Q18. Do you know the rules on part-time work for students?

Yes. I would need permission from Immigration first. After that the limit is 28 hours a week, and up to 8 hours a day in the long vacations.

Facts for you (S11, S13, S14):

  • The university's page starts: "International students are not allowed to work for a living." It gives up to 8 hours a day, up to 40 hours a week, in the spring, summer, winter and end-of-academic-year vacations.
  • No work in bars, clubs and similar places. No part-time work on a student visa before you enrol or after you finish. Working without permission can mean fines of up to 2 million yen. You must report any part-time job to the university.
  • TA or RA work inside Hiroshima University needs no separate permission. Don't ask Prof. Zheng for TA or RA work in this call.

Q19. Will you keep working for Layerpath from Japan?

No. I'll leave Layerpath before I enrol and study full time. [CONFIRM: Layerpath is not paying for my studies.]

Note for you: Immigration says freelance work, or any work whose hours are hard to check, needs a separate individual permission (S11, S12). So don't offer remote or freelance work as your backup plan.

Risks

Q20. What if the rupee falls against the yen?

[CONFIRM: I've planned my budget in yen and kept a margin of about 10% for the exchange rate.]

Note for you: at 0.603 rupees per yen, the two years come to about 2.0 to 2.3 million rupees. If a yen costs 10% more in rupees (about 0.66), the two years cost about 200,000 to 230,000 rupees more.

Q21. What if money runs short in the second year?

[CONFIRM: I've planned both years before I start, not only the first, and the money for year 2 is set aside before I come.] If something still goes wrong, I'd apply for the tuition exemption and scholarships, cut costs, and only then take permitted part-time work. I would not let it stop the research.

Note for you: year 1 needs about 1.78 to 2.02 million yen and year 2 about 1.50 to 1.74 million yen (table above).

Q22. What about health insurance and medical costs?

I'll join National Health Insurance at the city office when I arrive, because everyone living in Japan must have public health insurance. It pays 70% of medical costs. I'll also take the student insurance the university requires.

Facts for you:

  • Everyone living in Japan must join public health insurance, and up to age 69 you pay 30% of medical costs (S24).
  • In Higashi-Hiroshima the FY2026 National Health Insurance tax is never zero. The lowest is 22,800 yen a year for people not aged 40 to 64 (S23).
  • To get the low rate you must declare your income every year, even if it is zero. The university says this is around May (S25).
  • The university makes its liability insurance ("Inbound Insurance", taken with Gakkensai) compulsory for all international students (S26). [CHECK] the price.
  • You will also be in the National Pension system. There is a way to be excused from paying when income is low (S13). [CHECK] the student rules at the city office.

Paying for the experiment

Q23. Who pays for the API costs of your experiment?

In my proposal, the 820 runs cost about 420 to 590 US dollars at the per-task prices in Gloaguen et al. I'd ask whether it fits a lab budget, and if not, move more runs to the open-weight model. [CONFIRM: I can pay for the 120 screening runs myself.]

Note for you: this split is a reading of the proposal, which does not name the agents: 20 tasks x 3 runs x 2 agents, probably Claude Code and Codex with GPT-5.2. At the proposal's prices: 60 Claude Code runs x 1.15 to 1.51 = 69 to 91 dollars; 60 Codex runs (GPT-5.2) x 0.32 to 0.57 = 19 to 34 dollars; so about 88 to 125 dollars in total. Say a number only if you have checked it and will really pay it.

Q24. What if the models cost more than you planned?

That's possible. Zhang et al. report about 5 US dollars per task and condition with Claude Opus 4.6. [CONFIRM: I'd set a cost cap per run] and move more runs to the open-weight model, which cost 0.12 to 0.15 dollars per task in Gloaguen et al.

If he pushes ("Is 5 dollars a run right?"): "The same paper gives about 2,000 dollars for over 5,000 runs, so the 5 dollars is their rough figure. My own pilot will measure the real cost." If he pushes ("What is the most you would spend yourself?"): [FILL: a number in US dollars you would really spend, or say "I'd rather fit the study to the open-weight budget than guess a number."]

Q25. Do you expect the lab to pay for anything?

Not for my studies. My fees and living costs are mine to cover. For the API runs, my proposal only asks whether they fit a lab budget, and if they don't, I'll move more runs to the open-weight model.

Your job

At work I can build and run agents, but I can't yet fit and test reliability models on their failures. I want to learn that properly, through probability, statistics and reliability courses with a supervisor, and that needs full-time study. So I'll leave Layerpath before I enrol.

Why he asks: some applicants want a visa or a break, not research. He wants to hear a research reason.

Q27. You will give up a salary for two years. Is it worth it?

I think so. [CONFIRM: I've saved for this, and my family agrees.] [CONFIRM: I want to keep working on how far we can trust code that agents write,] and two years here is the best way I know to learn how to measure it.

No. The fees are the same at both universities, so money is not the deciding point. I'll decide on the research and the lab, and I'll tell you as soon as I decide.

If you apply to Nagoya's MEXT program and win it, you must not decline it (S28), so a Nagoya award would decide for you.
